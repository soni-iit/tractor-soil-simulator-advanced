    Field data, calibration and validation

    The textbook model (κ = 1) is optimistic: on the three Kim et al. (2022) plough trials it under-predicted rear slip by 45–60 %. The calibration multipliers below absorb unmeasured axle loads, tyre deformation, tillage-induced soil state and test definitions. They are specific to the tractor and soil they were fitted on.

    Model equations
    Bn = (CI·b·d / W) · (1 + 5δ/h) / (1 + 3b/d)
    Bias ply: GTR = 0.88 (1 − e−0.1 Bn)(1 − e−7.5 s) + 0.04
    Radial: GTR = 0.88 (1 − e−0.1 Bn)(1 − e−9.5 s) + 0.0325
    Bias ply: MRR = 1 / Bn + 0.04 + 0.5 s / √Bn    Radial: MRR = 0.9 / Bn + 0.0325 + 0.5 s / √Bn
    Calibration: CI → κCI·CI in Bn, MRR → κρ·MRR    (κ = 1 is the textbook model)
    NTR = GTR − MRR    TE = NTR (1 − s) / GTR
    z = [ pc / (kc/b + kφ) ]1/n · (1 + 4s)
    σz = pc [ 1 − (1 / (1 + a²/z²))ν/2 ]

    Single wheel: Brixius (1987) wheel-numeric traction model (basis of ASABE D497.7), with the bias-ply and radial coefficient sets, a Bekker pressure–sinkage relation for rut depth and a Fröhlich concentration-factor form for subsoil stress. Bn uses the load on one wheel with that tyre's width b and diameter d. Contact patch: net contact pressure pc = kp·inflation (kp default 1.3), gross footprint Ag = W/(pc·lug ratio) (lug ratio default 0.6), contact length L = Ag/(πb/4), tyre deflection from the chord relation L = 2√(dδ − δ²) giving δ/h; a measured footprint can replace the estimate. Stress is computed on the net area, a = √(A/π). Version 3 corrects the rolling-resistance term used in version 1 (1.2/Bn → 1/Bn) and replaces the fitted deflection relation.

    Wr = Wr0 + DP·hd/L    Wf = Wf0 − DP·hd/L
    2WD: DP = Wr·NTRr − Wf·MRRf(s = 0)
    MFWD, 4WD: DP = Wr·NTRr + Wf·NTRf
    sf = 1 − (1 − sr) / (1 + lead)
    η = DP·va / Σ(GTR·W·vt)    va = vt(1 − sr)

    Whole tractor: static axle loads come from the tractor mass and rear weight share, and the pull shifts load rearward by DP·hd/L (the drawbar pull is taken as horizontal, and tractor rolling-moment terms are ignored). On 2WD the front wheels roll freely and only add motion resistance. On MFWD the front wheels drive with a positive lead, so they slip more than the rear wheels. The 4WD option uses equal tyres on both axles with no lead. Front wheels are assumed to run in undisturbed soil, so rear wheels running in front-wheel ruts are not modelled. Front tyre options and default masses are typical values, not manufacturer data.

    Texture to parameters: cone index at the reference moisture rises with clay and silt, CIref = 900 + 15·clay + 4·silt (kPa, firm surface). The Bekker exponent falls and kc rises with clay, and moisture lowers CI exponentially at a rate that grows with clay and silt. Tilled and loose surfaces carry 0.5 and 0.28 of the firm cone index. Reference moisture (near field capacity) is 6 + 0.35·clay + 0.1·silt in % d.b.

    Atterberg limits are estimated from texture: PI = 0.65·clay + 0.06·silt − 2 (below 4 the soil is treated as non-plastic), PL = 12 + 0.22·clay + 0.10·silt, LL = PL + PI, SL = 0.55·PL, all in % d.b. Liquidity index LI = (w − PL)/PI. The limits classify the soil state and drive the warnings. They also scale the cone index: CI = CIref · fm · fa, where fm = exp[−β(w − wFC)] limited to 0.4–2.0, and fa = 1 up to the plastic limit, falling linearly to 0.3 at the liquid limit (LI = 1) and decaying toward zero above it. Non-plastic soils keep fa = 1. A measured cone-index profile replaces this estimate: its mean over 0–150 mm is used as CI for the selected surface condition, and the other two conditions are scaled by their firm, tilled and loose ratios.

    Indicative only. States have several textures. These assignments follow the broad soil groups (alluvial, black, red, laterite, arid) and typical surface texture, not a measured class map. Crops in brackets are the major crops grown on that soil. For comparison, an NBSS&LUP (ICAR) regional study of Tamil Nadu found sandy textures (sandy loam, loamy sand) dominant, followed by clayey and loamy soils (Kalaiselvi et al.).
